The Arta Card
Lending, designed like wealth.

Po Chun Lin · July 2026
A credit card launch for Arta's own members — and the platform behind it.
01 / 16
Opening

My read on the problem

  • Everything Arta does today asks "what do you want to grow?" — a card application asks "tell us about yourself so we can judge you."
  • New product archetype: lending brings forms, disclosures, decisioning, and the word "no" into a brand built on trust.
  • The real design problem: keep the premium, trust-forward feel through a flow that is legally obligated to interrogate and disclose.
02 / 16
Opening

Assumptions I made

Audience

Existing, KYC'd Arta members

Launch is on Arta's brand to its own members — not a cold-acquisition funnel.

Market

US launch, modular disclosures

HSBC SG form defines information scope, not jurisdiction. Disclosure block stays swappable.

Product

Premium rewards card

Rewards can sweep into investments. Underwriting may consider the Arta portfolio.

Platform

Mobile-first, in-app

Inside the existing iOS/Android app. Instant decisioning possible; manual review exists.

Three members, one flow

Grounded in Arta's real audience — tech professionals and accredited investors with a DIY spirit, many living partly on investment income. Every screen in this deck renders through one of these three; switch personas in the live prototype and the prefill, the questions asked, the decision, and the AI's judgment all change with them.

Persona 1 · The clean path

Alexandra Chen — salaried optimizer

Director of Engineering · $285K W2 · $412K with Arta.

Everything verifiable → instant approval. She proves the three-minute promise — and that the right AI for her is a quiet one.

Persona 2 · The friction test

Dev Patel — founder, lumpy income

Nimbus Labs · $180K drawn irregularly · $520K with Arta.

Stated income and records don't line up → docs-needed, with dignity. His AI reads cash-flow structure: statement date moved to meet his invoices.

Persona 3 · The differentiator

Margaux Laurent — portfolio-first

Investment income $95K · $2.35M with Arta.

Skips the employer questions; the portfolio toggle decides her limit ($40K vs. a $10K counter-offer) — and she gets the AI's richest intervention.

Flow 1 · The application

Thesis: confirm, don't collect

  • Arta already KYC'd this member — the flow confirms what Arta knows and asks only what it doesn't.
  • Four chapters, ~3 minutes: Confirm · Income · Terms · Review. Auto-saved, resumable.
  • Honesty is the premium experience: APR and fees appear before the funnel starts, not after.
Entry — pre-qualified card in the grid→ Confirm→ Income→ Terms→ Review + Key Terms→ Decision · 5 designed states

Every screen on the following slides is the live prototype embedded — three member personas drive the prefill, the questions asked, and the decision itself.

Terms & disclosures, unburied

S4 · Recommended limit preselected; full-balance autopay framed as the wealth-smart default
S5 · Key Terms never fold; consents separate and unchecked; full agreement opens as Arta’s own bottom-sheet legal pattern

Flow 1 · The application

The decision is a relationship moment

Approved — the newest card in your collection; virtual card usable now
In review — named stages, honest expectations, nothing to do
Declined — private, specific as legally possible, zero cross-sell

Pending, counter-offer and decline are designed — a declined applicant is still a wealth member tomorrow.

Flow 2 · AI across the lifecycle

One AI relationship, not scattered features

The in-product map: five moments, one anatomy — "you are here" at Manage
  • The card gives Arta AI the one signal it never had: cash flow. Arta saw what you own; now it sees how money moves.
  • Not a new mascot: Product Specialist extends to lending terms; Investment Planner gains cash-flow awareness.
  • One anatomy everywhere: a conclusion · the math · one tap · an exit. Chat exists behind "Ask about this" — never the default.

Dev — cash-flow structure: statement date moved to meet his invoices

12 / 16
Platform · White-label

Core stays fixed. Skin is the partner's.

Layer 1 · Locked

Platform core

Compliance flows & disclosure structure · decisioning · lending semantics (status / credit / decision / disclosure / AI colors) · legal type minimums · focus visibility.

Layer 2 · Contract

Product configuration

APR range, limits, rewards rule, SLAs — data consumed by screens, governed by contract, never themable.

Layer 3 · Partner

Brand expression

Palette, gradients & card art, radius, type family, link color — one theme file, zero component code.

The line is enforced by the token pipeline, not by review: a partner theme that re-points a decision color to its brand pink fails the build. One chain: #a8cdf0 → ref.color.sky.40 → sys.action.primary → cmp.btn.primary.bg — Arta is simply theme #1.

Closing

What's limiting in Arta's design system

Gradients ≠ forms

Aurora surfaces fight data entry. Reserve them for moments — approval, card art — and add a quiet form surface.

No legal type style

Letterspaced caps micro-labels fail for disclosures. Lending needs a readable legal/tabular style.

Missing primitives

Dense inputs, chapter progress, currency input, consent rows, status timeline, document upload, decision states.

And because a critique should be constructive: the missing layer now exists — 151 ref / 117 sys / 123 cmp tokens, the lending components, and three themes, running in a validated pipeline.
